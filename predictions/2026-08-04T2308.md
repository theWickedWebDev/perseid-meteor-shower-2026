## 2. The reasoning, briefly

**Member composition changes the answer before the weather does.** Night 1 currently has 6
members; ICON, UKMO and (for nights 2–3) NWS are simply out of range. By 9 Aug all three
reach all three nights. ICON and UKMO are the two most pessimistic sources on the
verification week (`mean_forecast` 85 and 79); NWS long-range sits around 51–63. Adding
three high members to a median currently held down by ECMWF (4%) and GFS (6%) moves it up
mechanically. Re-running today's Night 1 median with ICON/UKMO inserted near their weekly
means gives ~42 instead of 24 — **half my upward revision is arithmetic, not weather**.

**Regression to the base rate.** All three nights read better than the 65% climatological
median, by 41 / 33 / 7 points. Two of those anomalies are larger than the measured lead-7
error bar. Section 5 of the prompt is right about this and so is the project's own
`waiting_section`: expect them to shrink.

**Against those two, one real signal.** ECMWF and GFS both take Night 1 to single digits at
22–23h, and ECMWF is the best short-range model measured here (MAE 9 at 0–1 day). AIFS —
the most stable across the whole range, best at distance — reads 32 / 61 / 59 and is the
member I weight most at this lead. AIFS and the composition shift agree on the ordering:
**Night 1 clearest, Nights 2 and 3 mediocre.** I keep that ordering, with a compressed gap.

**Guess, flagged as such:** ICON at 85 and UKMO at 79 are weekly means from an overcast week,
not forecasts for the trip nights. If those two come in clear, every per-night number below
is 10–15 points too high.

## 4. Two things the data says that the documentation does not

**`consensus()` does not weight the core hours, and section 8 implies it does.**
`FORECAST_FINDINGS.md` §8 says hours are weighted by minutes the core is actually up
(0.35 / 0.28 / 0.22 for hour 23). `ensemble_trip()` and `climatology()` do apply
`core_weights()`. `consensus()` — the function that produces `night1/2/3`, the trend chart,
and the number the scorer grades — takes a **plain mean of hours 22 and 23**
(`log_forecast.py:635`). §2 scopes the weighting to "the ensemble", so §8 is loose rather
than false, but the headline percentage is not weighted and reads as though it is. On Night 1
today it is worth about 1 point; on a night that clears at 23:30 it would be worth much more.

**The observation that motivates the whole directional-cloud section is not in the log.**
§6 cites "4 Aug at 00:56: dome 54% cloud, north 0%, south 78%". The 00:00 entry in
`satellite_log.json` does carry `cloud: 54`, but its `octants` and `slot` are `null` — the
00:56 run was never banked. Of the **63 logged readings that do carry octants**, dome cloud
and the S/SW/SE octant mean agree to **0.8 points on average, 12.3 at worst**. The largest
north-south split ever banked is 33 vs 0 on a 7% night. So the stored evidence says the dome
number is an excellent proxy for the southern slot, and the one counterexample is
unreproducible. That does not make the compass wrong — the week was bimodal, 41 readings at
or above 50% and 10 at or below 9%, and a uniform sky is exactly where a proxy looks good —
but the case for it currently rests on a number that cannot be recovered.

## 5. The one missing measurement

**Score the models against the southern slot, not the whole dome.** `skill.py:118`
`truth_hours()` reads `e["cloud"]` — the 50 km dome average — and every number in the model
ranking, the lead-time curve, and the baselines table descends from it. But the trip does not
need the dome; it needs azimuth 177°–232° at 7–22° altitude, and `satellite.py` **already
banks that**: 63 of 74 log entries carry `slot: {near, mid, far}` and eight octants, at no
extra API cost. Adding a parallel `truth_hours(satlog, key="slot")` and emitting a
`lead_skill_slot` block is roughly fifteen lines and no new requests. It would answer a
question nobody has asked in numbers: *how much error does the whole-dome abstraction add to
the forecast that matters?* Today's answer looks like "almost none" (mean |dome − south| =
0.8), and if that holds up across a partly-cloudy night it retires the compass display as
decoration and simplifies the page. If it does not — if slot error runs 20 points above dome
error — then the entire "does waiting help" section is measuring the wrong quantity, and its
headline finding (17 points gained between lead 7 and lead 3) is smaller than the error the
page is not measuring at all.

## 6. What would show me wrong

- **Any per-night number below 25 or above 85 on 9 Aug.** My whole case is convergence
  toward the middle from both directions — climatology pulling up, pessimistic members
  joining. A night that reads 8% or 95% means the ensemble resolved decisively and my
  regression prior was the wrong instrument.
- **`joint` at 80 or above.** Then the verdict stays "a better draw", my headline call is
  wrong, and the 7–9 day ensemble was carrying real information rather than spread noise.
- **`joint` below 52.** Wrong the other way, and more interestingly: it would mean the
  ensemble collapsed onto a bad pattern faster than the base rate allows.
- **`spread1` below 45.** Never happened in 144 recorded night-readings. If it does, either
  the models genuinely agreed for the first time or the member set shrank — check `n` first.
- **Fewer than 8 members on any trip night.** A source that should be in range and is not is
  a plumbing failure, not weather.
- **`skill.json` climatology changed at all.** Nothing between now and the 9th should touch
  it. If it moves, either the ERA5 pull changed or `core_weights()` was edited.
- **`n_episodes` above 9.** Would mean the 7-day cap is not doing what I read it to do.
- **No `aod` on Night 3 by 9 Aug.** Then §10's stated timeline is a day optimistic and the
  page is blind on the pivot night's smoke until the 10th.
- **`night1` ≈ 24 and `spread1` ≈ 78 unchanged.** Not a forecast miss — that is the snapshot
  gate stuck, and it would mean nothing has been banked in five days.
