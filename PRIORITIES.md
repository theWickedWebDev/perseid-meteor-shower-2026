# Priorities — What This Trip Is Actually For

Read this before any schedule. When something has to give, this is what decides.

---

## The mission

> ## 1. Milky Way core
> ## 2. Perseid meteors
>
> **Everything else is extra.**

The refractor, the 6SE, the deep integration — all of it is bonus. Good bonus, worth hauling
1,000 lbs of equipment for, but bonus. If a night forces a choice, the choice is already made.

**Practical consequence:** the scope can't be left outside, so it can't image while you're at
the lake under any plan. It gets the back half of the night — **1:00 to 3:45** — on one target,
and if a night goes sideways you lose scope data and nothing that matters.

## The lake is not a detour — it's the same trip

**You're driving Route 3 north at dusk every night anyway to look for moose.** The lake is
where that drive ends. So there is no trade-off to agonise over and no reason to shoot the core
from the cabin at all:

- Leave ~7:15, work the moose corridor north through sunset
- Arrive the spot ~8:30, set up in civil twilight
- Core 9:56 – 11:25 PM at a **verified 204.4° bearing over a ~10° horizon**
- Back at the cabin ~12:10 for the meteor hours

**Every clear night follows this shape.** The cabin's low southern horizon stops mattering —
nice to know, but nothing depends on it now.

### What this means for the refractor

**The scope can't be left outside** — theft worry, however unlikely up there. So there is no
unattended imaging block at all. On lake nights the refractor's real window is:

> **Carried out at 12:15 → running by ~1:00 → 3:45 AM. Just under three hours.**

**The 3-season porch is what makes that work.** Unheated, so it tracks outdoor temperature.
The scope sits there from ~7 PM fully assembled and powered, camera cooling. By the time you
touch it at 12:15 the triplet is **already at ambient** — no thermal defocus, no 45-minute
settle — and the camera is already at −10 °C.

**Dew heaters stay OFF on the porch.** Under a roof the optics can't see open sky, so they
can't radiatively cool below the dew point and no dew forms. Heaters would only fight the
acclimation you're after. Switch them on when it goes out.

**The whole tripod moves assembled**, so alt/az and the head registration survive — but the
plate can shift and the ground settles. Budget a **polar align check with a small adjustment**
at 12:15, not a from-scratch alignment.

### The casualty: M8/M20

Below 10° by 12:30, so **the refractor can only reach it on night 1**, when you're at the
cabin anyway. One session, ~2.3 hours, and that's the blue pair done. Nights 2 and 3 get one
deep target each from ~1:00 to 3:45.

## Why the core outranks everything

You cannot point south from home. Not "it's harder" — the horizon isn't there. Every other
target on this list is available from the pier in September, October, or next spring, with as
many nights as you want and better calibration.

The core over water at Bortle 2 is the one image that only exists if you make it in this
90-minute window, on one of these three nights, at that spot.

**9:56 to 11:25 PM. Ninety minutes. That's the trip.**
