# OH SHIT — Sucker-Hole Triage

**It's been socked in all trip. The clouds just opened. What do you point at, right now?**

Read the decision tree, find the hour, go. Don't optimize. Don't deliberate.

---

## THE ONE RULE

> **Grab what's dying, not what's rising.**

Anything high and northern will still be there tomorrow, next week, or in October from the
pier. Anything low and southern is gone for a year. When in doubt, **point south and low.**

---

## Step 1 — How long is the break?

Reaction times are wildly different per system. Know them cold:

| System | Time to first useful frame |
|---|---|
| iPhone | **15 seconds** |
| Canon on fixed tripod | **2 minutes** |
| 6SE visual (alt-az, manual push) | **5 minutes** |
| Canon on SAM, tracked | **12–15 minutes** (polar align) |
| EQ6 + 76EDPH, from parked & aligned | **20–30 minutes** (unpark, slew, solve, focus, guide) |

| Break length | Do this |
|---|---|
| **< 15 min** | **Canon on a tripod. Nothing else.** Do not touch the EQ6 — you'll spend the whole break booting it and get zero frames. |
| **15–45 min** | Canon + 6SE visual. Scope only if it's *already* tracking something. |
| **45+ min** | Bring the refractor in. Worth the startup cost. |
| **Unknown / patchy** | Assume short. Canon first, scope second. You can always escalate. |

## Step 2 — Find the hour

### 9:00 – 10:00 PM · **SOUTH. EVERYTHING SOUTH.**

Astronomical dark starts 9:56. Before that, usable frames from ~9:30.

**Canon, 18mm or 35mm, aimed at azimuth ~195°.** One frame at 35mm gets Rho Oph, the
Milky Way core, and the Trifid together. This is the single richest hour of the entire trip
and it is the only hour that contains Rho Oph.

- Rho Oph: 13.2° and falling — **it dies at 10:30, permanently**
- Core: 15.1°, at its best
- M8/M20: 21.4°, at its best

**Scope:** M8/M20 if the break looks like it'll hold 45 minutes. Otherwise don't bother.

**Do not** point anything north this hour. Cepheus will keep.

---

### 10:00 – 11:00 PM · **STILL SOUTH. RHO OPH IS BLEEDING OUT.**

- Rho Oph: 10.1° at 10:30, below the treeline by 10:45. **Last call.**
- Core: 13.6° → 11.8°, crossing frame center around 11:00
- M8/M20: 20° → 18.6°

**Canon south, landscape orientation, immediately.** If you only get 20 minutes, this is
still the best 20 minutes available all trip.

**Scope:** M8/M20. It has until 12:30, so it's the safest scope target if the break holds.

---

### 11:00 PM – 12:00 AM · **CORE'S LAST HOUR. THEN SWITCH.**

- Core: 11.3° → 5.8°. **Hard deadline 11:21 / 11:17 / 11:13 PM, nights 1 / 2 / 3.**
- M8/M20: 18.6° → 13.4°, still fine
- Veil transits 12:17 AM at 75.6°

**Before 11:25 — Canon on the core, PORTRAIT.** The band is standing vertical out of the
southwest now. This is the postcard shot and it expires in minutes.

**After 11:25 — south is finished for wide-field.** Move the Canon to the meteor field
(radiant 32°, climbing) and put the scope on M8/M20 or Veil.

---

### 12:00 – 1:00 AM · **HANDOFF HOUR**

- Core: gone
- M8/M20: 13.4° → 10°, **dies at 12:30**
- **Veil transits 12:17 AM at 75.6° due south**

**Scope:** M8/M20 only if you can be capturing by 12:10. Otherwise go straight to the
**Veil** — high, bright, forgiving, and starting it just after transit means no meridian
flip for the rest of the night.

**Canon:** meteor field. Radiant climbing through 32–38°.

---

### 1:00 – 2:00 AM · **HIGH AND EASY**

Nothing is dying. Everything left is high, so pick for quality, not urgency.

**Scope:** Veil (transiting west, 75° → 65°), or Cocoon + B168 (transits 1:19 AM at 87.8°,
essentially overhead — immune to any horizon problem). Dark Shark if north is clear.

**Canon:** meteor field, Cassiopeia/M31 framing. Radiant 38° → 47°.

---

### 2:00 – 3:00 AM · **PERSEID PRIME**

- Radiant 47° → 55°. Rates are ~2.5× the evening.
- M31 66° → 75°

**Canon on meteors, no question.** 20s, f/1.8, ISO 3200, continuous.

**Scope:** Veil or Cocoon, unattended.

**6SE:** NGC 7331 + Stephan's Quintet while it's high — Bortle 2 in a six-inch is the only
way you'll ever see the Quintet. Then M31, M33, Saturn (~39°, rings near edge-on).

**And put the eyepiece down for fifteen minutes and just watch.** You drove four hours.

---

### 3:00 – 3:45 AM · **METEORS ONLY. DON'T BUILD ANYTHING.**

- Radiant 55° → 61° — **best rates of the night**
- Astronomical twilight begins 3:43 AM

Forty-five minutes is not worth an EQ6 startup. **Canon on a tripod, 20s, f/1.8, go.**
Lawn chair. Look up.

---

### After 3:45 AM

Twilight. Done — **unless there's aurora.** See below.

---

## Step 3 — Overrides

### AURORA AT ANY HOUR — DROP EVERYTHING

Pittsburg sits near 55° geomagnetic. Kp5 puts glow on the northern horizon; Kp6–7 puts
structure overhead. It is the rarest thing on this entire list and it does not wait.

**Canon, 18mm, f/1.8, 5–10s, ISO 1600, pointed north.** Reflection on water if you can get
to it. iPhone as a second camera — modern Night Mode handles aurora well.

Abandon whatever the scope was doing. It'll still be there next month.

### A HOLE OVER ONE PART OF THE SKY ONLY

Point at the hole. Consult the hour above only to break ties. A clear patch beats an
optimal target every time.

### BREAK ARRIVES WHILE YOU'RE AT THE LAKE

Stay. The lake is the only southern-horizon-over-water access you have, and the cabin can't
substitute for it. The scope can lose a night; the core over water cannot be rescheduled.

## Step 4 — Readiness posture (do this EVERY cloudy evening)

**This is the part that actually determines whether you catch a sucker hole.** A break you
can't react to in five minutes is a break you didn't get.

- [ ] **EQ6 polar aligned, powered, parked at home/Polaris.** Do this at dusk regardless of
      forecast. Polar alignment is the long pole and it doesn't care about clouds.
- [ ] **2600MC cooled to −10°C and holding.** Cooldown is 10 minutes you won't have.
- [ ] **Capture sequence loaded, Scheduler job built.** One click from running.
- [ ] **Note the EAF step count** from the last good focus. If it clears for eight minutes
      you do not want to be running a full V-curve.
- [ ] **Canon on the tripod, lens mounted, UV/IR filter on, focused, settings dialed.**
      Cap it, don't disassemble it.
- [ ] **Dew heaters ON even under cloud.** Humidity under overcast is brutal. Soaked optics
      when the break comes cost you the break.
- [ ] **Batteries in, cards in, dummy battery plugged in.**
- [ ] **Dashboard running** on the phone so you can check rig state without going inside.
- [ ] **This file printed or open on the phone.**

## The 5-minute scramble

In order. Don't improvise.

1. **Look at the clock.** Find the hour above.
2. **Uncap the Canon. Point it.** Frames start accumulating while you do everything else.
3. **Check where the hole actually is** — override the plan if the clear patch is elsewhere.
4. **Decide on the scope**: is this break going to last 45 minutes? If unsure, no.
5. **If yes** — unpark, slew, plate-solve, restore last focus position, start guiding, capture.
6. **Only then** think about the 6SE.

---

## What NOT to do

- **Don't chase perfect focus** on a five-minute break. Slightly soft frames exist. Zero
  frames don't.
- **Don't start a meridian flip** into a closing hole.
- **Don't reframe a working composition** because you thought of a better one.
- **Don't shoot north in the first two hours of dark.** Ever. On this trip, that sky is free
  and the southern sky is not.
- **Don't tear down at the first re-clouding.** Sucker holes travel in groups. Cap the optics,
  leave everything aligned, wait.
