# Schedule — One Clear Night · THE GRAB BAG

Two nights lost. **Don't try to build a stacked masterpiece of anything.** Collect one good
version of every shot on the list and go home with a full set instead of one deep target and
a lot of regret.

Fewer frames each. Move on before you're finished. That's the whole strategy.

## The decision, made in advance

**Go to the lake.** Do it anyway:

- The core over water is the one image you cannot make anywhere else, ever
- The southern window is 9:56–11:25 PM regardless of where you stand
- The scope can't be left outside, so it can't image while you're gone under *any* plan —
  there's nothing to give up by leaving
- Stay at the cabin instead and you get M8/M20 on the refractor and drive home without the
  picture you came for

**The scope gets the back half.** Assemble it on the **unheated 3-season porch** in the
afternoon — powered, camera cooling, dew heaters off. It acclimates while you're at the lake,
comes out at 12:15 already at ambient, and runs one target from **1:00 to 3:45**. No polar
alignment needed beyond a rough one, since you're not chasing sub-arcsecond guiding in three
hours.

**Take the 6SE with you.** On a one-night trip the southern showpieces — M8, M17, M16, M22, M11 —
only exist between 9:56 and midnight, and the lake has the horizon for them. It gives your
friend something real to do while you're working, and the fork mount travels fine.

## Lake kit

**Four separate supplies, one per system — deliberately.** No single point of failure: a blown
fuse or a bad cable takes out one thing, not the session.

| Load | Power |
|---|---|
| **Canon** | **LP-E17 batteries.** Swap at the **10:45 landscape→portrait break**, not when it dies |
| **Dew strap** | **USB power bank** — ~5 h off 10,000 mAh |
| **6SE** | **Lead-acid direct**, 12 V, no regulator. Its native supply |
| **SAM Mini** | **2×AA — lithiums, plus a spare pair.** The USB option is in the power box, which stays in the truck |
| Everything else | Power box, coupler, spare cables ride in the truck as **backup** |

The alligator lead only feeds the 6SE at ~1 A now, so gauge barely matters — but still
**fuse the positive lead at the battery end** (5 A) and **verify centre-positive with a meter**
before first use.

### Optics
- Canon T8i + Sigma 18-35, **72 mm UV/IR filter fitted**, lens hood
- SAM Mini + tripod — **latitude wedge set to 45°**
- Something to drop the tripod to **~0.5 m** for the creative session
- **6SE + fork + tripod + eyepieces + 32 mm Plössl.** **Leave the EQ wedge home** — it does
  nothing for visual and costs setup time you don't have
- NexStar handset **site set to 45° 14' N / 71° 12' W**, not your home coordinates
- iPhone + mini tripod as a second wide camera

### Power
- Lead-acid battery — **let it rest off the charger**, it reads 14.4 V fresh
- DIY power box + input cable
- DR-E18 coupler · 12 V barrel cable for the 6SE · USB-C cables
- All four LP-E17 batteries, charged the day before — reserve, since the 7.4 V output carries
  the session
- ⚠️ **Inline fuse (5–10 A) on the positive lead, at the battery end.** A lead-acid shorts at
  hundreds of amps; the box's own fuse doesn't protect the cable feeding it
- **Verify polarity with a meter** — centre positive. **18 AWG or heavier**; thin leads sag and
  the 6SE throws "No Response" errors
- USB dew heater strap, **labelled 5 V**
- USB power bank as backup · chemical hand warmers as backup-backup

### Everything else
- Spare SD cards, IR thermometer gun
- Two headlamps — one red for working, one warm-white for light painting · amber gel
- Chairs, layers, bug spray
- **ID** — Border Patrol works that corridor

## The one thing to remember

You are collecting, not perfecting. **Twenty frames and move on.** A trip with one good version
of eight different shots beats a trip with ninety subs of a nebula you can photograph from your
own backyard in October.
