# THE BLUES

## M8 Lagoon + M20 Trifid — one frame, every night

`M20: 18h 02m 23s / −23° 02'` · `M8: 18h 03m 37s / −24° 23'`

**1.38° apart**, M20 almost due north of M8. Both fit easily. **Centre RA 18h 03m, Dec −23° 42',
long axis N–S.**

**M20 is the red-and-blue object** — red Ha lobe south, distinct **blue reflection lobe** north,
dark lanes splitting the red into three. M8 is mostly red Ha with the bright Hourglass. Bonus in
frame: **M21** (blue-white cluster, 0.7° NE) and **NGC 6559 / IC 4685** (another red/blue/dust mix).

| Time | Alt | Az |
|---|---|---|
| 9:56 PM | 21.4° | 186° |
| 11:00 PM | 18.6° | 202° |
| Midnight | 13.4° | 216° |
| 12:30 AM | 10° | 222° |

**120 s subs**, not 300 — sky fills fast at 20° and M8's Hourglass clips.

> ### Shoot the blue first
> At 15–21° you're at 3–4 airmasses, and extinction hits blue roughly twice as hard as red. The
> Trifid's reflection lobe comes in suppressed. **The first hour — 9:56 to 10:45, at 21° down to
> 20° — is your blue. Later frames are red bonus.**

> ### Break your usual recipe here
> `FIELD_OPS.md` says skip PCC because it washes pale reflection nebulae to white. **Not on this
> one.** The red-versus-blue contrast *is* the subject, your full-spectrum body is already
> pushing red hard, and extinction has skewed the balance further. This is the
> **`platesolve` → `pcc` → `satu` → stretch** path your own notes flag as accurate-colour-with-punch.

## IC 5146 Cocoon + Barnard 168 — Night 2

`21h 53m 24s / +47° 16'`

**The best colour target of the trip.** Red Ha core, **blue reflection around the rim**, and a
long black dust river (B168) trailing west. Red, blue, and grey in a single frame. The complex
runs ~2° — comfortable in 3.94° with the lane along the long axis. **Not in your capture history.**

**Transits 1:19 AM at 87.8° — essentially zenith.** No altitude penalty, no horizon risk in any
direction, minimal extinction, so the blue actually survives.

> ⚠️ **Near-zenith meridian flips are the awkward ones** — azimuth swings fast and clearance gets
> tight. Start it at 12:30 and supervise the 1:19 flip. Or start at 1:25, just past transit, and
> track down the west side with no flip at all — costs 55 minutes, buys certainty.

## vdB 152 "Wolf's Cave" + Dark Shark — Night 3

`vdB 152: 22h 13m 27s / +70° 15'` · `LDN 1235: 22h 14m / +73° 20'`

A bright **blue reflection knot** at the end of a dark dust lane, sitting **3.08° from Dark
Shark** — essentially the same RA, 3° south in declination.

**Centre at Dec +71° 47', long axis N–S**, and both cores land in one frame.

> **Honest caveat:** centre-to-centre is 3.08° and the long axis is 3.94°, but Dark Shark itself
> runs 1.5–2° and vdB 152's lane adds another degree. The full combined structure is more like
> 4.5–5°, so **one frame gets both objects but clips the outer dust on each.**
>
> Three options: (a) centre between them and accept the clipping — still blue *and* grey in one
> image, (b) a **two-panel mosaic** with 20% overlap covers ~7° and you have the hours for it,
> (c) Dark Shark alone, full extent.
>
> **Take (a) on a first attempt.** Mosaics complicate the Scheduler and the processing, and a
> clipped two-object frame beats a perfect one-object frame for what you're after.

# THE OTHER COLOURS

## Veil / Cygnus Loop — Night 1

`20h 51m / +30° 40'`

**Teal OIII and crimson Ha in braided filaments** — a completely different palette from the
reflection nebulae. 3° object in a 3.94° field: Eastern Veil, Western Veil, and Pickering's
Triangle all in one frame. Capture history has only NGC 6974, a single segment.

**Transits 12:17 AM at 75.6° due south**, finishes 3:45 AM at 47.2° / azimuth 269°. Entirely
inside the SE→WNW arc, never below 47°, above the ~25° WNW treeline throughout.

> **Start it a few minutes after transit and it's already west of the meridian — no flip all
> night.** Ideal for night 1 when you're still learning what breaks.

## LDN 1235 "Dark Shark" — Night 3

`22h 14m / +73° 20'` · Cepheus

**Grey and brown dust, and it's the shapes that carry it.** The "dark/dusty nebula (TBD which
one)" from the deep-integration list in `remote-astro/CLAUDE.md`. A pure dark-sky target —
reflection-and-dust structure that Bortle 5 simply does not render.

Northern, but **never below 52.7°** — clears the cabin's ~17° NW treeline easily.

| | |
|---|---|
| Transit | 1:39 AM at **61.8°, due north** |
| 10 PM | 52.7°, az 023° |
| Midnight | 59.6°, az 014° |
| 3:30 AM | 59.1°, az 345° |

**Circumpolar does not avoid a meridian flip** — a GEM still flips at upper culmination. What
Dec +73 gives you is generous tripod clearance, so set a large meridian limit deliberately.

# CANON — wide-field

## Lake: the southern Milky Way

**One 35 mm frame gets Rho Ophiuchi, the Milky Way core, and the Trifid together** — they span
~22°, the frame is 35.3° wide. And Rho Oph is the biggest colour target of the trip: **blue
reflection at Rho Oph itself, gold around Antares, red Ha, black dust threading through.**

- **9:56 – 10:45 PM** — band diagonal → **landscape**, 18 mm
- **11:00 – 11:25 PM** — band vertical out of the SW → **portrait**, rising from the water
- Aim **azimuth 204°**

Rho Oph dies at 10:30. Core dies at 11:25. Hard deadlines.

The astromod matters most here — full spectrum with the UV/IR cut pulls Ha along the whole
Sagittarius arm in a way a stock body cannot.

## Cabin: Perseids + Cassiopeia/M31

Verified in Stellarium. 18 mm landscape, radiant ~7° inside the lower-left corner. In frame:
Cassiopeia–Cepheus Milky Way, Wizard Nebula, Caroline's Rose, M103, **M31 with M32 and M110**,
Double Cluster, **Deer Lick Group** (NGC 7331 + Stephan's Quintet), Blue Snowball, NGC 752.

**Aim point at 2 AM: altitude 58°, azimuth 71° (ENE).**
**Field test:** frame it so Perseus is just out of shot. If the Double Cluster appears, back off.
**Settings:** 20 s, f/1.8, ISO 3200, continuous, tracked on the SAM.

# 6SE VISUAL

Max true field ~1.0° (1.25" only, 1500 mm).

**The southern showpieces only exist 9:56 PM – midnight and are invisible from home:
M8, M17 Swan, M16 Eagle, M22, M11 Wild Duck, M20.** That's the "why did we drive here" moment.
Since you're at the lake in that window, **bring the 6SE along** — the fork travels fine and the
lake has the horizon for it.

Later: NGC 7331 + **Stephan's Quintet** (the trophy — Bortle 2 in a six-inch is what makes it
visible), M31, M33 for spiral structure, Cat's Eye, Blinking, Saturn Nebula, M57, M27, M13, M92,
M15, M2, **Albireo** (gold and blue double — colour, visually), Epsilon Lyrae.

**Saturn** ~39° at 2 AM, rings near edge-on after the 2025 ring-plane crossing.
**Jupiter unavailable** — near solar conjunction.
